Difference Between Custom and Chemist or Sports Mouthguards

By Footscray Dental Studio Team · 20 April 2026 · Updated 16 September 2026

Walk into any chemist or sports store in Melbourne and you can walk out with a mouthguard for under twenty dollars. Book an appointment with a dentist and you will pay considerably more and wait a week or two. Both products go in your mouth and both claim to protect your teeth, so the obvious question is whether the difference justifies the gap. This guide from Footscray Dental Studio compares custom dentist-fitted mouthguards with chemist and sports shop alternatives across fit, protection, durability and cost, and sets out who each type genuinely suits.

Why Mouthguard Choice Matters More for Athletes

Sport puts the teeth and jaw under a particular kind of stress. Athletes deal with continuous force from clenching, with pressurised tooth-on-tooth contact, and with the accidental blows that come with contact play. All three happen repeatedly across a season.

A mouthguard that does not fit well, or that is made from lower quality material, can shift or displace during high-momentum activity. When that happens the consequences go beyond protection. A guard that moves can interfere with breathing and cause irritation, both of which affect performance in training and competition. An athlete thinking about their mouthpiece mid-play is not thinking about the game.

A mouthguard that does its job needs to mould precisely to the individual's dental profile and stay firmly in place through impact and movement. Just as importantly, it needs to distribute shock and impact force evenly across the teeth and jaw rather than concentrating it in one spot, because concentrated force is what causes significant injury.

The practical implication is that function and fit should outrank appearance when choosing. Colour and branding are the easiest things to judge in a shop and the least relevant to whether your teeth survive a collision.

Chemist and Sports Mouthguards

Over-the-counter mouthguards are designed for basic tooth protection. They are readily available from sports goods stores, chemists and online retailers, and their appeal is immediate: you can have one in your bag the same afternoon.

Most are made from thermoplastic materials that soften when placed in hot water. You bite into the softened material and it takes a rough impression of your teeth, which is where the common name "boil-and-bite" comes from. The flexible composition provides an approximate fit rather than a precise one.

They suit people who need something quickly, who are not especially concerned about durability, and who are not expecting a perfect fit. Convenience and immediate availability are their genuine strengths, and for occasional casual use they are considerably better than no protection at all. Guards sold by retailers such as Rebel Sport are typical members of this category.

Common Reasons Athletes Choose Chemist Mouthguards

1. Low Upfront Cost

Over-the-counter mouthguards typically cost between AUD $7 and $55 or more, depending on brand and construction. For someone starting out in a sport, or for a family outfitting several children at once, that is a meaningful difference. They are a reasonable option for beginners working to a tight budget.

2. Immediate Availability

You can buy one on the spot from a chemist or sports goods shop. No appointment, no waiting period, no second visit. When a season starts on Saturday and the realisation arrives on Friday, that matters.

3. No Professional Assessment Required

Boil-and-bite guards are moulded at home following the instructions on the packet. There is no dental visit involved, which appeals to people who prefer convenience or who are hesitant about dental appointments generally.

Limitations of Chemist and Sports Store Mouthguards

The trade-offs are real, and they concentrate in three areas.

1. Fit and Comfort Problems

Home moulding produces an approximate fit at best. The material cannot capture the fine detail of your teeth and gum contours the way a laboratory-made guard does.

The practical result is that these guards often loosen during intense physical activity, exactly when you need them most. They also tend to be bulkier than custom alternatives, and a bulky guard that has shifted position can cause difficulty with breathing and speaking. Athletes frequently end up clenching to hold the guard in place, which introduces its own problems.

2. Durability and Wear

The thermoplastic composition wears faster than the materials used in custom fabrication. As the material compresses and deforms with use, the guard loses its grip on the teeth.

That declining grip means declining protection and increasing discomfort over the life of the guard. Many over-the-counter mouthguards need replacing within a single season, which narrows the cost gap more than the shelf price suggests.

3. Compromised Safety

The most significant limitation is structural. An approximate fit cannot distribute impact force evenly across the jaw and teeth, because it is not in consistent contact with them. Force that should be spread across the whole arch ends up concentrated at whichever points the guard happens to touch.

The result is reduced protection and an elevated risk of injury compared with a properly fitted alternative. Injuries that a good guard would have absorbed, including chipped and cracked teeth, become more likely. Our guide to cracked teeth explains why those injuries are so often harder to treat than they first appear.

What Is a Custom Dentist-Fitted Mouthguard?

A custom mouthguard is engineered precisely to the individual's dental profile. Rather than approximating the shape of an average mouth, it reproduces the actual contours of your teeth and bite.

That precision delivers three things together: an accurate fit, a high standard of protection, and comfort that allows you to forget you are wearing it. For sportspeople who take safety seriously, that combination is the whole point.

Custom guards also suit people who grind their teeth frequently, and those wearing orthodontic appliances who need protection that accommodates hardware rather than fighting it.

The trade-off is availability. These are not retail products sitting on a shelf. A custom mouthguard is made by a dentist, which means visiting a clinic for the impressions or scan and returning once it has been fabricated.

How Custom Mouthguards Are Made

1. Dental Assessment

Fabrication begins with an assessment of your dental profile. Your dentist examines your teeth, bite and gums, and takes impressions or a digital scan of your mouth. This determines the appropriate type and thickness of guard for your sport and your circumstances.

A rugby forward, a nightly grinder and a junior netball player do not need identical guards, and the assessment is where that distinction is made.

2. Laboratory Fabrication

The impressions, bite records and details of your dental profile go to a dental laboratory. A skilled technician constructs the mouthguard on a model of your mouth using high-grade materials, typically acrylic resins and copolymers chosen for their impact absorption and durability.

This is a fundamentally different manufacturing process from heating a preformed shell in a saucepan, and the difference shows in how the finished guard performs.

Reasons Athletes Choose Custom Mouthguards

1. Regular Sports Participants

For anyone involved in high-intensity sports with direct impact, including MMA, karate, boxing and contact football codes, a custom guard shields the teeth from blows and potential injuries in a way an approximate fit cannot.

The effectiveness comes down to that even distribution of shock across the arch. A force absorbed and spread by a well-fitted guard is a force that does not concentrate on a single tooth.

Custom guards cost more than chemist options, typically in the range of AUD $150 to $400 in Australia. For regular participants, the calculation usually favours the custom option once replacement frequency is taken into account.

2. Habitual Grinders and Clenchers

Custom mouthguards are not only for sportspeople. They are commonly recommended for people who grind or clench their teeth at night, a category often described as bruxism mouthguards or, more plainly, a mouthguard for grinding teeth.

Worn overnight, a custom guard takes the wear that would otherwise fall on your enamel. This helps prevent the chipping and flattening that sustained grinding causes, and it preserves natural tooth structure that cannot be regrown. If you wake with jaw soreness or aching teeth, our article on tooth pain relief covers how grinding-related discomfort presents and what else can cause it.

3. Patients Who Have Had Dental Restorations

Anyone with crowns, implants, bridges or orthodontic appliances benefits from a guard built around their actual dental work. A custom mouthguard accommodates existing restorations properly rather than pressing awkwardly against them.

This matters because restorations represent a real investment. If you have had crowns or bridges or dental implants placed, protecting them during sport is straightforward and considerably cheaper than repairing them afterwards.

Custom Versus Chemist Mouthguards Compared

Setting the two side by side across the factors that matter:

  • Availability. Chemist and sports guards are available over the counter from chemists, sports stores and online. Custom guards are made at a dental clinic and require an appointment.
  • Fit and comfort. Over-the-counter guards offer an approximate fit that may loosen during play. Custom guards provide a precise, secure fit built to your dental profile.
  • Protection level. Over-the-counter guards deliver basic protection. Custom guards deliver high-level impact protection with even force distribution.
  • Durability. Over-the-counter guards wear out faster and often need replacing within a season. Custom guards are made from more robust materials and last considerably longer.
  • Cost. Over-the-counter guards range from around $7 to $55 or more. Custom guards typically range from $150 to $400.
  • Best suited for. Over-the-counter guards suit beginners and casual, occasional athletes. Custom guards suit regular athletes, habitual grinders, and anyone with braces, crowns or other restorations.

Which One Is Better for Athletes?

For athletes participating regularly, custom-fitted mouthguards are the stronger choice, and the reasons stack up consistently.

They provide a precise fit aligned to your dental profile, which means they stay comfortable and stay put across a full match rather than needing constant readjustment. They offer a higher standard of protection against both tooth injuries and soft tissue cuts to the lips and cheeks. They distribute shock across the teeth and jaw as intended rather than concentrating it. And they offer the durability to withstand heavy, repeated impact through intense activity without degrading.

The higher price point is genuine, but so is the longer service life. Set against replacing an over-the-counter guard several times a season, and against the cost and inconvenience of treating a dental injury, a custom guard represents sound value over time. That said, suitability depends on an individual assessment, and the right answer for a weekend social player may reasonably differ from the right answer for a competitive one.

Children's Mouthguards for Sports

Participation in high physical-contact sports among Australian children aged roughly nine to twelve has grown steadily, including in disciplines such as MMA and boxing alongside the traditional football codes.

Without proper protection, developing teeth are vulnerable. An injury sustained while the teeth and jaw are still growing can disrupt the developing dental profile and create complications that persist well into adulthood, which is a much longer shadow than most parents expect from a single knock.

Over-the-counter children's mouthguards are widely stocked by pharmacies and sports shops, and many parents start there because children outgrow equipment quickly. That is a practical starting point for casual play, though the same fit limitations apply, and they apply more sharply to a mouth that is still changing shape. For children playing regular contact sport, having the fit checked by a dentist is worthwhile, and a custom guard is often the better option despite needing replacement as the teeth develop.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a custom mouthguard and a sports mouthguard?

A custom mouthguard is professionally fitted to your teeth from impressions or a digital scan, which gives it superior protection and comfort. A sports mouthguard, including chemist and over-the-counter options, has a generic fit and may not fully protect against impact. Sports versions from brands such as Shock Doctor, Rebel and Rhino are convenient and inexpensive, but they cannot match the precision of a custom solution.

How do you mould a mouthguard at home?

Home moulding applies to boil-and-bite types sold in stores and chemists. You soften the mouthguard in hot water following the packet instructions, then bite down firmly so it shapes around your teeth as it cools. Products such as Polident retainer and mouthguard items often include home-moulding or cleaning guidance. A professionally made custom mouthguard removes the trial and error entirely.

How do you clean a mouthguard?

Rinse it with cool or lukewarm water immediately after use to remove saliva and debris, since hot water can distort the material. Brush it gently with a soft toothbrush and mild soap or a non-abrasive toothpaste, then rinse thoroughly and allow it to air dry completely. Store it in a ventilated case, which prevents bacteria building up in a damp, enclosed space.

Where can you buy a mouthguard?

Pharmacies and sporting goods stores stock over-the-counter and boil-and-bite options, and online retailers carry a wide range of sports mouthguards from well-known brands. Custom-fitted mouthguards are made by dentists and tailored to your own teeth, so they come from a dental clinic rather than a shop.

Are custom mouthguards better than Rebel or Rhino mouthguards for sports?

Custom mouthguards are designed specifically for your teeth, which allows them to offer greater protection, comfort and durability during high-impact activity. Rebel and Rhino mouthguards provide affordable sports protection and are a reasonable entry point for casual participation. What they lack is the tailored fit and long-term reliability of a dentist-made custom guard.

Can I use Polident retainer and mouthguard products together with my guard?

Polident products are primarily for cleaning and maintaining oral appliances, so they do not replace a mouthguard or add any protection themselves. They can be used to care for a custom mouthguard, helping keep it hygienic and extending its usable life. Always check that a cleaning product is suitable for your particular guard material.

Why choose a custom mouthguard over an off-the-shelf sports mouthguard?

Custom mouthguards are moulded specifically to your teeth, which translates into better protection against dental injuries, improved comfort and greater durability. Generic options such as Shock Doctor guards or standard sports mouthguards are convenient and inexpensive, but they compromise on fit, and fit is what determines how effectively impact is absorbed and distributed.
