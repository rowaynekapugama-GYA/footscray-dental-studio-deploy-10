8 Easy Steps to Overcome Dental Anxiety Before Visiting a Dentist

By Footscray Dental Studio Team · 20 April 2026 · Updated 16 September 2026

If the thought of sitting in a dental chair makes your stomach knot, you are in very good company. Australian research suggests almost 16% of Australians experience high dental fear, and many more feel at least a flutter of nerves before an appointment. The trouble is that anxiety keeps people away from care, and the longer the gap between visits, the bigger the problems waiting when they finally return. At Footscray Dental Studio we see nervous patients every week, and we can promise you this: dental anxiety is common, nothing to be embarrassed about, and very manageable. Here is what it is, where it comes from, and eight practical steps that genuinely help.

What Is Dental Anxiety?

Dental anxiety, known in its severe form by the scientific name odontophobia, is the fear or unease associated with dental visits and treatment. It ranges widely in severity, from mild butterflies in the waiting room to intense distress that leads people to delay or avoid treatment altogether, sometimes for years.

Common symptoms include:

  • Restlessness in the days leading up to an appointment
  • A racing heart, nausea or shortness of breath at the thought of the visit
  • Fear of pain from needles, instruments and dental procedures

If any of that sounds familiar, keep reading. Understanding where the fear comes from is the first step to loosening its grip.

Common Causes of Dental Visit Anxiety

1. Past negative experiences

Unpleasant experiences have a way of persisting in memory. A painful teeth whitening session or a difficult procedure from two or three years ago can be enough to trigger hesitation every time an appointment rolls around. Even second-hand experiences count: witnessing or hearing about someone else's painful dental visit can amplify your own anxiety, despite the fact that you were never in the chair.

2. Sensitivity to sounds and smells

For some people the fear is sensory. The sound of dental instruments, or the distinctive odour of procedural liquids, causes real discomfort. What makes this worse is mental rehearsal: in the days before an appointment, anxious patients often replay those sounds and images in their minds, and this vicarious rehearsal steadily builds treatment-related fear before they have even left home.

3. Embarrassment about oral health

This one is under-acknowledged but remarkably common. Patients who feel self-conscious about the appearance of their teeth or about bad breath often fear being judged as negligent about their personal hygiene. That judgment anxiety quietly translates into dental phobia, and people put off the very visit that would resolve the thing they are embarrassed about. For the record: dentists have seen everything, and our job is to help, not to judge.

8 Practical Tips to Overcome Dental Visit Anxiety

1. Acknowledge your anxiety

The first step in managing dental fear is simply admitting it exists. Naming the fear puts you back in control of your nerves and lets you prepare for any discomfort rather than being ambushed by it. Telling our reception team you are a nervous patient when you book also lets us plan a gentler, unhurried visit.

2. Practise relaxation techniques

Controlled breathing is one of the most reliable ways to release tension and restore a sense of normality. Try this: sit somewhere soothing, close your eyes and let your body relax. Breathe in through your nose for seven seconds, hold the breath for seven seconds, then exhale slowly through your mouth for seven seconds. A few rounds of this before and during your appointment can noticeably lower your stress levels.

3. Use distraction methods

Distraction is highly effective during treatment. Bring earbuds and a playlist of your favourite music; redirecting your attention to something pleasant genuinely rewires your focus away from the procedure. Visual distraction works too, so ask whether a movie or show can play on a screen while you are treated. Patients often find distraction reduces how much discomfort they perceive, even during longer procedures such as root canal treatment.

4. Bring a support person

A close friend or family member in the room, or even just in the waiting area, provides real consolation. A support person can chat with you before your appointment, keep your mind occupied, and stop tension from escalating while you wait.

5. Visualise positive outcomes

Instead of dwelling on the appointment itself, picture what comes after it: a confident, bright smile, or eating your favourite foods without pain. Think of treatment like a challenging race with a prize waiting at the finish line. That mindset keeps fear from overwhelming you during the procedure, because your attention is anchored to the outcome rather than the moment.

6. Consult a psychologist

For intense dental phobia, professional help is a sensible and effective route. A psychologist can assess the severity of the phobia and recommend proven coping mechanisms, including Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) to rewire distressing thought patterns, exposure therapy to gradually reduce the power of fear-causing triggers, and relaxation or hypnosis techniques guided by a trained therapist. There is no shame in this step; severe phobia is a recognised condition, and it responds well to treatment.

7. Communicate openly

Building rapport with your dentist changes everything. Before treatment starts, agree on a simple hand-raise gesture that means "please pause". Knowing you can stop the procedure at any moment restores a sense of control, and it lets your dentist ease instrument pressure the moment anything feels uncomfortable. At our Barkly St practice we build this kind of communication into every appointment, because a patient who feels heard is a patient who can relax. We focus on keeping you comfortable at every step.

8. Schedule wisely

Choose appointment times that work with your natural rhythms rather than against them. Early morning appointments are often best, because stress hormone levels tend to be lower then than at midday, and an early visit leaves the rest of the day for recovery and rest. Booking a low-stakes first visit helps too: our $135 New Patient Exam & Clean (which includes an examination, clinical photos, an intraoral scan, X-rays, a scale and clean, fluoride and a treatment plan) is a gentle, pressure-free way to get reacquainted with the dental chair. If anxiety runs in the family, starting children early with calm, positive visits through our children's dentistry service helps prevent dental fear from ever taking hold.

You're Almost Ready for Your Visit

With these coping techniques in hand, you are far better equipped than most nervous patients who walk through our doors, and it may finally be time to book that treatment you have been putting off. The right environment matters as much as the techniques: care from a reliable, empathetic dental team makes each visit easier than the last, and small wins build on each other. A straightforward check-up and clean is the ideal starting point, and if you are curious what routine care involves financially, our guide to the cost of dental cleaning in Australia walks through it without surprises. Footscray Dental Studio is right here on Barkly St in Footscray, our dentists practise with empathy and use techniques that limit discomfort, and we would be glad to show you that a dental visit can be a genuinely calm experience.
