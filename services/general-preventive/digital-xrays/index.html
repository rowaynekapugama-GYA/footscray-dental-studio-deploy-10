General & Preventive Dentistry

Digital X-Rays / Diagnostics

Accurate diagnosis is one of the most important parts of effective dental care — and much of what affects your oral health isn't visible to the naked eye.

Book an Appointment
Overview

What you should know

Decay between teeth, issues below the gum line, bone health, and the position of unerupted teeth all require imaging to assess properly. At Footscray Dental Studio, we use digital X-ray technology to capture detailed images quickly and efficiently, helping your dentist make well-informed decisions about your care.

Digital X-rays produce images almost instantly, using significantly less radiation than traditional film X-rays, and can be viewed and enlarged on-screen so your dentist can talk you through exactly what they show.

What's Involved

Diagnostic imaging at our practice may include:

  1. Bitewing X-rays — used to check for decay between teeth and monitor bone levels around teeth.
  2. Periapical X-rays — focused images of a specific tooth, from crown to root tip, often used when investigating pain or infection.
  3. OPG (Orthopantomogram) imaging — a panoramic view capturing the entire jaw, teeth and surrounding structures in a single image, useful for broader assessments including wisdom teeth, jaw joints, and treatment planning.
  4. Intraoral camera images — small camera-captured images of the mouth used alongside X-rays to give a complete picture of your oral health.

Your dentist will explain which type of imaging is appropriate for your specific situation and why.

Benefits of Digital X-Rays

  • Significantly lower radiation exposure compared with traditional film X-rays
  • Immediate results, viewable on-screen during your appointment
  • Greater diagnostic accuracy for issues not visible during a standard exam
  • Images can be enlarged and reviewed together with your dentist
  • Supports more precise treatment planning for procedures such as root canal therapy, implants, and orthodontics

When X-Rays May Be Recommended

Your dentist may recommend diagnostic imaging if you're experiencing dental pain, as part of a new patient examination, before certain treatments (such as implants, root canal therapy, or wisdom tooth assessment), or as part of your regular preventive check-up schedule.

Is this right for you?

Who It's Suitable For

X-rays are recommended based on individual clinical need, which your dentist will assess as part of your check-up or treatment planning. This applies to patients of all ages, with imaging tailored to each person's specific requirements.

What to Expect

The process is quick, non-invasive and generally comfortable. Your dentist or dental assistant will position the equipment carefully and let you know what to expect beforehand. Images are available for review within moments.
