Dental Implant Procedure: A Step-by-Step Guide to What to Expect

By Footscray Dental Studio Team · 28 January 2026 · Updated 16 September 2026

Replacing a missing tooth with an implant is a staged process rather than a single appointment, and knowing what each stage involves takes a great deal of the uncertainty out of it. This guide from Footscray Dental Studio walks through the dental implant procedure from the first consultation to the day the final crown is fitted, including how long each phase typically takes and what influences the timeline.

What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that is placed into the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Once it has integrated with the bone, it provides a stable foundation for a crown that looks and functions much like a natural tooth.

Implants are versatile. A single implant can replace one tooth, several implants can replace multiple teeth, and a small number of implants can support a full-arch restoration. Our pages on dental implants and All-on-X implants explain how those options differ.

Initial Consultation and Treatment Planning

Everything starts with an assessment. At the first appointment the dentist reviews your medical history and general health, examines your teeth, gums and overall oral health, and takes imaging, usually X-rays or a scan, to assess the bone levels in the area where the implant would sit.

Just as importantly, this is the conversation where your goals and expectations are discussed. From all of that, a personalised treatment plan is created, taking into account the anatomy of your mouth, the strength of the bone available and the health of the teeth on either side. Footscray Dental Studio offers a complimentary implant consultation, including for All-on-X, which you can read about on our special offers page.

Preparing the Implant Site

Before an implant can be placed, the dentist needs to be confident there is enough healthy bone to support it. Two preparatory steps sometimes come first.

Tooth extraction, if needed

If a damaged tooth is still in place, it may need to be removed before implant surgery. In some situations an implant can be inserted on the same day as the extraction. In others, a healing period is required first so that the site settles before the implant goes in. Which approach suits you depends on the condition of the bone and the surrounding tissue at the time.

Bone graft, when required

Where the available bone is inadequate, a dentist or specialist may recommend a bone graft. The purpose of a graft is to rebuild the jawbone so that it can provide a strong foundation for the implant. Healing from a graft can take several months, depending on individual circumstances and the size of the area being rebuilt. Our article on bone grafting for a tooth implant covers this step in more detail for anyone who has been told they may need one.

Dental Implant Surgery

Implant placement is a carefully planned procedure carried out under local anaesthetic, with additional comfort measures available where appropriate. The gum tissue is gently opened, a precise space is prepared in the bone, and the implant is placed. A surgical guide is often used to improve the accuracy of the positioning. The gum is then closed over or around the implant.

Most people find the appointment shorter than they expected. The emphasis throughout is on comfort and safety, and while everyone's experience differs, most patients report only mild discomfort in the days afterwards, managed with simple pain relief.

Healing and Osseointegration

This is the critical phase. Osseointegration is the process by which the implant fuses with the jawbone. Bone grows around the titanium surface until the implant becomes genuinely stable and secure, while the gum tissue heals around the surgical site.

The timeframe varies from person to person but typically runs to several months. Good oral care during this period reduces the risk of complications, which can include gum disease around the site or, less commonly, failure of the implant to integrate. The Better Health Channel has useful general information on implants and what healing involves.

Abutment Placement

The abutment is a small connector piece that sits between the implant and the crown, providing the support structure the final crown attaches to.

Placing it may involve reopening the gum slightly so the abutment can be attached to the implant, after which the gum heals around the component. In some cases the abutment is placed during the initial surgery rather than at a separate appointment, which removes one visit from the schedule.

Temporary Crown, If Needed

While the final restoration is being made, a temporary crown may be fitted. It maintains appearance and function during the fabrication period and protects the implant site in the interim.

A temporary crown is not built for heavy chewing forces, so it is best treated gently. Think of it as a bridge between stages rather than a finished result.

Creating the Permanent Crown

The final tooth is fabricated by a dental laboratory and matched to the surrounding teeth in shape and colour, which is what allows the finished result to look natural rather than obviously replaced.

Once ready, the permanent crown is attached to the abutment. From that point it functions much like a real tooth and blends with the rest of your smile.

How Long Does Each Stage Usually Take?

  • Initial consultation and planning: typically one visit
  • Implant placement surgery: usually completed in a single appointment
  • Healing and bone integration: several months
  • Abutment and crown fitting: one or two further visits

These timeframes vary depending on the health of your bone, whether grafting is needed and how your body responds to healing. The staged approach exists for a reason: allowing the implant to bond securely before it is asked to support a crown reduces the risk of complications and supports a more predictable outcome. Spacing the treatment over time is a feature of the process, not a delay in it.

Risks and Considerations

Dental implants are generally very successful, but like all surgical procedures they carry a degree of risk. Several factors increase the chance of implant failure, including smoking, uncontrolled gum disease and certain medical conditions.

A good dentist will discuss these risks with you clearly before you commit, and will give you guidance on how to protect the implant once it is in place. Suitability for implant treatment and the results achieved depend on an individual assessment, so an honest conversation at the planning stage is worth far more than any general promise.

Life After Dental Implant Placement

Once healed, an implant supports normal chewing, clear speech and, for many people, a noticeable return of confidence. With good care, implants can last many years and contribute to ongoing dental and bone health by keeping the jawbone stimulated where a tooth was lost.

Maintaining that result comes down to the same habits that protect natural teeth: regular check-ups, thorough daily oral hygiene, and following the advice of your dental professional. Keeping the rest of your mouth healthy matters too, since untreated decay elsewhere can undermine an otherwise successful result. If decay is part of your history, our guide on how to fix rotten teeth explains how treatment options change at each stage.

Final Thoughts

The dental implant procedure follows a structured plan designed to restore missing teeth with a natural-looking outcome that stays stable over time. It involves several stages, and each one exists to support long-term function and comfort rather than to draw the process out.

If you are considering implants, the sensible first step is a consultation with a qualified dentist who can assess whether the treatment suits your individual needs and explain the process clearly. For patients across Footscray, Yarraville and Melbourne's inner west, that conversation happens at our Barkly St practice. If you are weighing an implant against saving a tooth you still have, our breakdown of root canal cost in Australia may help you compare the two paths.

Please note: Any surgical or invasive procedure carries risks. Before proceeding, you should seek a second opinion from an appropriately qualified health practitioner.
