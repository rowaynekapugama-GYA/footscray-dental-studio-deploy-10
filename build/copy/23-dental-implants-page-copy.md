Dental implants are a long-term option for replacing missing teeth, consisting of a titanium post surgically placed into the jawbone, which acts as an artificial tooth root. Once integrated with the bone, a custom crown or bridge can be attached, restoring both function and appearance.

## What's Involved

The dental implant process generally includes:

1. **Consultation and assessment** — including a discussion of your goals and a clinical examination, often supported by X-rays or a scan to assess bone structure.
2. **Treatment planning** — your dentist develops a plan tailored to your situation, which may involve referral to or collaboration with a specialist for the surgical placement.
3. **Implant placement** — the titanium implant post is placed into the jawbone under local anaesthetic.
4. **Healing period** — a period of healing allows the implant to integrate with the surrounding bone (a process known as osseointegration).
5. **Restoration** — once healed, a custom crown or bridge is attached to the implant, completing the restoration.

## Benefits of Dental Implants

- Designed to function similarly to a natural tooth root
- Doesn't rely on adjacent teeth for support, unlike a traditional bridge
- Can help maintain jawbone structure in the area of the missing tooth
- Restorations are custom-made to match your natural teeth

## Who It's Suitable For

Dental implants may be suitable for patients missing one or more teeth who have sufficient bone density and good general oral health to support the implant. Certain factors — such as smoking, some medical conditions, or insufficient bone volume — may affect suitability, which your dentist will assess during your consultation. Where bone volume is a concern, additional procedures may be discussed as part of your treatment plan.

For patients missing most or all of their teeth in an arch, our **[All-on-X Dental Implants](/services/restorative/all-on-x-implants/)** page may be more relevant.

## What to Expect During Recovery

Some swelling or discomfort is common in the days following implant placement, which your dentist will help you manage. A healing period is required before the final restoration is attached, allowing time for the implant to properly integrate with the jawbone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does the dental implant process take?**
This varies depending on individual healing time and the complexity of your treatment plan — your dentist will provide an estimated timeline during your consultation.

**Is getting a dental implant painful?**
The procedure is carried out under local anaesthetic, and your dentist will discuss pain management for the recovery period, which varies by individual.

**Am I a suitable candidate for dental implants?**
Suitability depends on factors such as bone density, gum health, and general health — this is assessed during a consultation, which is complimentary at Footscray Dental Studio.

**How long do dental implants last?**
With appropriate care and maintenance, implants are designed for long-term use — your dentist can discuss what to expect based on your individual circumstances.

**Do you place implants in-house or refer to a specialist?**
Please contact our team to confirm current arrangements, as some aspects of implant treatment may involve collaboration with a specialist depending on case complexity.

**What if I'm missing most or all of my teeth?**
For patients missing most or all of their teeth in an arch, All-on-X may be a more suitable option — see our dedicated All-on-X page for details.
