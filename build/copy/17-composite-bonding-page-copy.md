Composite bonding uses a tooth-coloured resin material, carefully applied and shaped to address minor cosmetic concerns such as small chips, gaps, or irregularly shaped teeth. It's a more conservative option than veneers, as it typically requires little to no removal of natural tooth structure, and can often be completed in a single visit.

## What's Involved

Composite bonding at our practice generally includes:

1. **Consultation** — your dentist assesses your teeth and discusses whether bonding is a suitable option for your concerns.
2. **Shade matching** — the composite resin is matched to the natural shade of your surrounding teeth.
3. **Application** — the resin material is applied in layers and shaped to achieve the desired result.
4. **Curing** — each layer is hardened using a curing light.
5. **Shaping and polishing** — the bonded material is shaped and polished to blend naturally with your existing teeth.

## Benefits of Composite Bonding

- Often completed in a single visit
- Minimal to no removal of natural tooth structure required
- A more conservative and reversible option compared with veneers
- Can be used to address small chips, gaps, or shape irregularities

## Who It's Suitable For

Composite bonding may be suitable for patients with minor cosmetic concerns who are looking for a more conservative treatment option. It may not be suitable for larger corrections or more significant concerns, where veneers or other treatments might be more appropriate. Your dentist will assess your teeth and discuss the most suitable option for your goals.

## Caring for Bonded Teeth

Composite material can be more prone to staining and chipping than porcelain over time, so your dentist may provide specific guidance on diet and care to help maintain your bonding. Regular check-ups allow your dentist to monitor the condition of the bonded areas.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does composite bonding take?**
Many bonding treatments can be completed in a single visit, though this depends on the number of teeth involved and the extent of the work.

**Is composite bonding painful?**
Bonding is generally a comfortable procedure, often requiring little to no anaesthetic — your dentist will explain what to expect for your specific treatment.

**How long does composite bonding last?**
Longevity varies between patients depending on habits such as diet and teeth grinding — your dentist can discuss what to expect and how to help it last.

**Can composite bonding be repaired if it chips?**
In many cases, minor chips to bonded areas can be repaired — your dentist will assess this on a case-by-case basis.

**Is bonding a permanent treatment?**
Bonding is generally considered a more conservative and reversible option compared with veneers, though it will need monitoring and maintenance over time.

**What's the difference between bonding and veneers?**
Bonding uses a resin material applied directly to the tooth with minimal preparation, while veneers are custom-fabricated porcelain shells requiring some tooth preparation — your dentist can advise which is more suitable for you.
