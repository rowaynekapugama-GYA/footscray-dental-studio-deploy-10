## H1: Fissure Sealants

**Breadcrumb:** Home > Services > General & Preventive Dentistry > Fissure Sealants

The chewing surfaces of your back teeth (molars and premolars) contain natural grooves and pits, known as fissures. These areas can be deep and narrow enough that a toothbrush bristle can't fully clean them, making them particularly vulnerable to decay. Fissure sealants are a thin, protective coating applied to these surfaces, acting as a barrier against plaque and food particles.

At Footscray Dental Studio, fissure sealants form part of our preventive approach, particularly for children as their adult molars come through.

## What's Involved

Applying a fissure sealant is a quick, straightforward process:

1. **Cleaning and preparation** — the tooth surface is thoroughly cleaned and dried.
2. **Application** — a liquid sealant material is applied into the grooves of the tooth.
3. **Setting** — the sealant is hardened, often using a special curing light, forming a smooth protective layer.
4. **Check** — your dentist checks your bite to ensure the sealant feels comfortable.

No drilling or anaesthetic is required, making it a comfortable option, including for younger patients.

## Benefits of Fissure Sealants

- Creates a protective barrier over hard-to-clean tooth surfaces
- Quick and painless to apply, with no drilling involved
- Can reduce the risk of decay in molars and premolars
- Particularly useful for children as their adult teeth emerge
- A cost-effective, preventive alternative to treating decay after it occurs

## Who May Benefit Most

Fissure sealants are commonly recommended for:

- Children, as their permanent molars erupt (generally around ages 6 and 12)
- Patients with deep grooves or pits in their back teeth
- Patients with a history of decay in molars or premolars
- Adults at higher risk of decay who haven't previously had sealants applied

## Who It's Suitable For

Fissure sealants are commonly recommended for children as their adult molars come through, though they may also be suitable for adults at higher risk of decay in these areas. Your dentist will advise if sealants are appropriate for you or your child during an examination.

## Caring for Sealed Teeth

Sealants support—but don't replace—good oral hygiene. Continued brushing, flossing and regular check-ups remain important, and your dentist will monitor the condition of sealants at future visits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**At what age are fissure sealants usually applied?**
They're commonly applied as children's adult molars erupt, typically around ages 6 and 12, though the right timing depends on individual dental development.

**Are fissure sealants only for children?**
No, adults at higher risk of decay in these areas may also be suitable candidates, depending on individual assessment.

**Does the process hurt?**
No, applying fissure sealants is quick and non-invasive, with no drilling or anaesthetic required.

**How long do fissure sealants last?**
Your dentist will monitor sealants at regular check-ups and let you know if they need attention or reapplication over time, as wear can vary between patients.

**Do sealants replace the need for brushing and flossing?**
No, sealants are an additional preventive measure and don't replace regular oral hygiene at home.

**Can sealants be applied during a regular check-up visit?**
Yes, they're commonly applied as part of a routine preventive visit, making it a convenient addition to your check-up.
