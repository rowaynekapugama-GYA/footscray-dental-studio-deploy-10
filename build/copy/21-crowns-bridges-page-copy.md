## H1: Crowns & Bridges

**Breadcrumb:** Home > Services > Restorative Dentistry > Crowns & Bridges

Crowns and bridges are custom-made restorations used to protect, strengthen or replace teeth. A crown covers and protects a damaged or weakened tooth, while a bridge replaces one or more missing teeth by anchoring to the surrounding teeth. Both are designed to restore function and blend naturally with your smile.

## What's Involved

**Dental Crowns**
1. **Assessment** — your dentist examines the tooth and discusses whether a crown is the appropriate treatment.
2. **Preparation** — the tooth is shaped to accommodate the crown, removing any decayed or damaged material.
3. **Digital scan or impression** — used to fabricate a crown that fits precisely.
4. **Temporary crown** — may be fitted while your permanent crown is being made, if fabricated off-site.
5. **Fitting** — your custom crown is fitted, checked for comfort and bite, and permanently cemented into place.

**Dental Bridges**
1. **Assessment** — your dentist assesses the gap and the health of the surrounding teeth that will support the bridge.
2. **Preparation** — the supporting teeth are prepared to hold the bridge.
3. **Digital scan or impression** — used to fabricate a bridge custom-fitted to your bite.
4. **Fitting** — the bridge is fitted, checked, and cemented into place.

## Who It's Suitable For

- **Crowns** may be recommended for teeth that are cracked, heavily decayed, worn, or following root canal treatment, where a filling alone wouldn't provide sufficient strength or protection.
- **Bridges** may be recommended for patients missing one or more teeth who have healthy, suitable teeth on either side of the gap to support the bridge. For patients without suitable supporting teeth, or who prefer not to prepare adjacent healthy teeth, a dental implant may be discussed as an alternative.

Your dentist will assess your individual situation and discuss which option is most suitable.

## Caring for Crowns & Bridges

Crowns and bridges are cared for like natural teeth — regular brushing and flossing, along with check-ups, help your dentist monitor their condition and the health of the supporting teeth and gums underneath.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long do crowns and bridges last?**
Longevity varies by individual factors such as oral hygiene, bite forces and material used — your dentist can discuss what to expect for your specific restoration.

**Does getting a crown or bridge hurt?**
Local anaesthetic is used during preparation, and most patients find the process manageable — your dentist will explain what to expect for your treatment.

**How many visits does it take to get a crown or bridge?**
This depends on whether your crown or bridge is fabricated on-site or by an external laboratory — your dentist will explain the expected timeline during your consultation.

**What's the difference between a bridge and an implant?**
A bridge replaces a missing tooth by anchoring to the neighbouring teeth, while an implant is anchored directly into the jawbone without involving adjacent teeth — your dentist can discuss which suits your situation.

**Can a crown be placed on a tooth that's had a root canal?**
Yes, a crown is often recommended following root canal treatment to protect and strengthen the tooth.

**What materials are used for crowns and bridges?**
Your dentist can discuss the material options suitable for your specific situation, based on the location and function of the tooth involved.
