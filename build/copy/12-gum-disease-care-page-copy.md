Healthy gums are just as important as healthy teeth, providing the foundation that supports your teeth in place. Gum disease develops in stages — starting with gingivitis, an early, generally reversible inflammation of the gums, which can progress to periodontitis if left untreated, potentially affecting the bone and tissue supporting your teeth. Regular check-ups allow your dentist to identify early changes and recommend appropriate care before the condition progresses.

At Footscray Dental Studio, gum health is assessed as a standard part of every check-up, with treatment tailored to the stage and severity of any gum disease present.

## What's Involved

Depending on your individual assessment, gum disease care may involve:

1. **Assessment** — your dentist examines your gums for signs of inflammation, bleeding, recession or pocket depth around the teeth.
2. **Professional cleaning** — removal of plaque and tartar build-up, including below the gumline where relevant.
3. **Home care guidance** — practical advice on brushing, flossing and other techniques to improve gum health day-to-day.
4. **Ongoing monitoring** — your gum health is reviewed at subsequent visits to track progress and adjust care as needed.
5. **Referral where appropriate** — for more advanced periodontal disease, your dentist may recommend referral to a periodontist for specialised treatment.

## Benefits of Early Gum Disease Care

- Gingivitis is generally reversible with timely professional care and improved home hygiene
- Early intervention can help prevent progression to more serious periodontal disease
- Supports long-term retention of your natural teeth
- Reduces the risk of bone and tissue loss associated with advanced gum disease
- Contributes to overall oral comfort and confidence

## Signs of Gum Disease to Watch For

- Bleeding gums, particularly when brushing or flossing
- Red, swollen or tender gums
- Persistent bad breath
- Gums that appear to be receding, making teeth look longer
- Loose or shifting teeth (a sign of more advanced disease)

## Who It's Suitable For

Gum disease care is relevant to any patient showing signs of gingivitis or periodontal disease. Regular check-ups help identify these signs early, often before they're noticeable to the patient themselves, which is one of the key reasons routine visits are recommended even without symptoms.

## Supporting Healthy Gums Long-Term

Good home care plays an important role in maintaining gum health between visits, including:

- Brushing gently but thoroughly, twice daily
- Daily flossing or interdental cleaning
- Avoiding tobacco use, which is a significant risk factor for gum disease
- Attending regular check-ups so changes can be caught early

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the early signs of gum disease?**
Common signs include bleeding gums, swelling, and sensitivity, though your dentist can identify early changes during examination that aren't yet noticeable to you.

**Is gum disease reversible?**
Early-stage gum disease (gingivitis) can often be managed effectively with improved home care and professional cleaning; more advanced periodontal disease may require ongoing management rather than a complete reversal.

**How often should my gum health be checked?**
Gum health is typically assessed as part of your regular six-monthly check-up, though your dentist may recommend more frequent monitoring if you have existing gum concerns.

**Does gum disease treatment hurt?**
Your dentist will discuss what to expect based on your specific treatment plan and can take steps to help manage any discomfort during treatment.

**Can gum disease affect my overall health?**
Your dentist can discuss the relationship between oral and general health as it relates to your individual circumstances during your consultation.

**Will I be referred to a specialist for gum disease?**
Depending on severity, your dentist may recommend ongoing care in-house or referral to a periodontist for more advanced periodontal treatment.
