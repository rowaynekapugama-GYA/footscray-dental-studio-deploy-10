## What Counts as a Dental Emergency

Common dental emergencies include:

- Severe or persistent tooth pain
- A knocked-out (avulsed) tooth
- A broken, cracked or chipped tooth
- A lost filling or crown
- Swelling in the face, gums or mouth
- Bleeding that doesn't stop after an injury
- A broken denture or orthodontic appliance causing discomfort

If you're unsure whether your situation counts as an emergency, please call us — our team can help assess the urgency and advise on next steps over the phone.

## What to Do While You Wait to Be Seen

**Knocked-out tooth**
Handle the tooth by the crown (the white part), not the root. If possible, gently rinse it without scrubbing and try to reinsert it into the socket. If that isn't possible, keep it moist — in milk or your saliva — and get to us as soon as possible, as timing can affect the chance of saving the tooth.

**Severe toothache**
Rinse your mouth with warm water. A cold compress against the outside of your cheek may help with discomfort. Avoid placing aspirin directly against the gum or tooth.

**Broken or chipped tooth**
Rinse your mouth with warm water and, if possible, save any broken tooth fragments. A cold compress can help reduce swelling.

**Lost filling or crown**
If you still have the crown, keep it safe and bring it with you. Avoid chewing on the affected side until you're seen.

**Swelling or facial trauma**
Apply a cold compress to the outside of the affected area and contact us promptly, as swelling can sometimes indicate infection requiring urgent attention.

*This guidance is general in nature and doesn't replace professional dental or medical advice. If you're experiencing severe symptoms, difficulty breathing or swallowing, or significant facial trauma, please seek emergency medical care immediately.*

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should I do if my tooth gets knocked out?**
Handle it by the crown, avoid touching the root, and try to reinsert it into the socket if possible. If not, keep it moist in milk or saliva and come in as soon as possible — timing matters for the best chance of saving the tooth.

**Do you offer same-day emergency appointments?**
We aim to see emergency patients as promptly as possible within our opening hours — please call ahead so our team can prepare for your visit.

**What if I have a dental emergency outside your opening hours?**
Please contact your nearest hospital emergency department or after-hours medical service for urgent care outside our opening hours.

**Is a toothache always an emergency?**
Severe or persistent pain should be assessed promptly, as it can indicate infection or other issues needing timely treatment — call us and our team can help assess urgency.

**Can a knocked-out tooth always be saved?**
This depends on factors including how quickly it's treated and how the tooth was cared for in the meantime — prompt attention gives the best chance of a positive outcome.

**Should I go to a hospital or a dentist for a dental emergency?**
For most dental emergencies, contacting our practice directly is the right first step. For severe trauma, difficulty breathing or swallowing, or significant blood loss, please seek emergency medical care immediately.
