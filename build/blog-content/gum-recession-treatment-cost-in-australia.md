If your dentist has mentioned gum recession, one of your first questions is probably a practical one: what is this going to cost? Prices for gum recession treatment in Australia vary enormously, from a few hundred dollars for a professional deep clean to several thousand for surgical grafting, so it pays to understand what drives the numbers before you commit to anything. This guide sets out the typical Australian price ranges for each type of treatment, the factors that push costs up or down, and the extras that are easy to overlook. All figures below are general market ranges, not the fees of any particular clinic, and your actual quote will always depend on an individual assessment. If you want the background on why gums recede in the first place, start with our [complete guide to gum line recession](/a-go-to-guide-on-gum-line-recession-causes-and-remedies/).

## Factors Affecting the Cost of Gum Recession Treatment

Two patients can walk into the same clinic with "receding gums" and leave with quotes that differ by thousands of dollars. That is not inconsistency; it reflects genuine differences in how far the recession has progressed and what it will take to manage. Four main variables explain why one patient pays a few hundred dollars and another pays several thousand.

### 1. Severity of the Gum Recession

Clinicians generally group recession into three stages, and each stage carries a different price bracket:

- **Mild recession** is usually managed with professional scaling, cleaning and ongoing monitoring. Typical costs sit under AUD $1,000.
- **Moderate recession** may need more intensive scaling or periodontal treatment carried out over multiple visits, with typical costs of AUD $1,000 to $2,000 or more.
- **Advanced recession** often requires gum grafting or regenerative surgery, which are complex procedures typically costing AUD $2,000 to $3,000.

Staging matters because it determines not just the procedure but the number of visits, the amount of anaesthetic and materials involved, and whether a general dentist or a specialist periodontist carries out the work. The pattern is clear: the earlier recession is caught, the cheaper it is to deal with. That alone is a good financial argument for regular check-ups and the [daily habits that prevent recession](/preventing-gum-recession-effective-daily-habits-to-adopt/) in the first place.

### 2. Type of Treatment Required

Treatment type is partly tied to severity, but it is worth pricing separately:

- **Non-surgical treatments**, including scaling, root planing and laser-assisted therapy, typically cost up to AUD $500 to $550 per quadrant (a quadrant being one quarter of the mouth).
- **Surgical treatments**, including gum graft surgery, flap surgery and regenerative procedures, typically range from AUD $600 up to $5,000 or more for complex cases.

Because treatment type is linked to severity, you cannot really choose the cheap option off a menu. What you can do is act early, while the non-surgical bracket is still open to you.

### 3. Location and Clinic Reputation

Where you are treated matters. Clinics in major cities such as Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane generally charge more because their operating costs, from rent to salaries, are higher. Metropolitan pricing can run 20 to 50 per cent above the baseline, while rural and suburban clinics tend to be comparatively affordable. The clinician's experience also plays a part: a periodontist with five or more years of specialist experience will usually command higher fees, and that expertise generally correlates with better procedural outcomes.

### 4. Insurance Coverage

If you hold private health insurance with extras cover, you may receive a partial rebate on periodontal treatment. How much comes back depends on your policy type, your insurer, your policy tier and your annual benefit limits. Be realistic here: no extras policy fully covers periodontal treatment, so expect partial coverage and some out-of-pocket cost. Medicare does not generally cover adult dental treatment either. It is worth phoning your insurer with the item numbers from your treatment plan before you book, so you know your gap in advance rather than at the reception desk.

## Common Gum Recession Treatments and Their Costs

Here is what each treatment involves and what it typically costs across Australia. Treatment almost always starts at the conservative end of this list and only escalates if the gums genuinely need more.

### Non-Surgical Treatments

#### Deep Cleaning (Scaling and Root Planing)

Deep cleaning is scaling and root planing performed below the gumline, where an ordinary toothbrush and even a standard clean cannot reach. It removes the plaque and tartar that contribute to gum recession, reduces inflammation and helps the gums reattach snugly to the teeth. It is usually the first treatment recommended, and for early recession it is often the only one needed. Typical Australian costs are AUD $400 to $700 per quadrant, or AUD $1,200 to $2,500 and up for full-mouth treatment. Depending on how much tartar has built up below the gumline, the work may be spread over two or more appointments so each area can be treated thoroughly and comfortably. This kind of periodontal therapy is part of the [gum disease care](/services/general-preventive/gum-disease-care/) we provide at Footscray Dental Studio.

#### Antimicrobial Treatments and Laser Therapy

Deep cleaning is sometimes paired with adjunctive therapies to knock down bacterial infection. These include antibacterial gels placed into gum pockets, antimicrobial rinses, and laser periodontal therapy offered at some clinics, all used to clean gum pockets and remove infected tissue. They are add-ons to a deep clean rather than stand-alone cures, so factor them in on top of the scaling fee if your dentist recommends them. Typical costs are AUD $50 to $300 per session for antimicrobial treatments and AUD $500 to $1,000 per quadrant for laser periodontal therapy.

### Surgical Treatments

#### Gum Grafting

Gum grafting is the procedure most people picture when they think of "fixing" receded gums. A dentist or periodontist takes tissue from a donor site, most often the roof of the mouth, and secures it over the worn areas of the gumline. Common graft types include connective tissue grafts, free gingival grafts and pedicle grafts, and the choice between them depends on how much tissue is missing, how thick your existing gum is and where in the mouth the recession sits. Typical Australian costs run from AUD $600 to $2,000 or more per tooth or site, and AUD $2,000 to $5,000 or more where multiple teeth or complex cases are involved. Grafting is a surgical procedure typically performed by a periodontist; Footscray Dental Studio does not perform it, but we can assess your gums and discuss whether a surgical opinion is the appropriate next step. Whether grafting is worth it in your case ties back to a bigger question we explore in [is gum recession reversible?](/is-gum-recession-reversible-what-orthodontics-say/)

### Pinhole Surgical Technique

The pinhole technique is a newer, minimally invasive alternative in which the clinician makes small entry points in the gum and gently repositions the existing tissue over the exposed roots. Its advantages are minimal suturing, reduced recovery time and short healing periods, which is why it appeals to patients who want to avoid a traditional graft. Those benefits come at a price: the average Australian cost range is AUD $3,000 to $6,000 or more, depending on the extent of treatment. It is offered by a limited number of trained clinicians, so availability varies by area, and not every case is suitable for the technique.

### Additional Costs to Consider

The procedure fee is not quite the whole story, and quotes that look cheap up front sometimes leave these items out. Budget for the following as well when comparing your options:

#### Follow-Up Visits

Post-treatment reviews are crucial for monitoring healing and heading off infection before it can undo the work you have paid for. Expect AUD $100 to $300 per visit, depending on complexity, and plan for at least one or two reviews after any significant periodontal treatment. Skipping them to save money is a false economy.

#### Medications

Depending on the treatment, you may be prescribed antibiotics or anti-inflammatory medication afterwards. As a rough guide, allow around AUD $100 in additional expense.

## Comparing Treatment Costs at a Glance

Because tables of figures can blur together, here is a side-by-side summary of the typical Australian ranges for the main procedure types, from the most contained to the most extensive:

- **Gum grafting (per tooth or site):** AUD $600 to $2,000+ per tooth
- **Multiple-tooth or complex grafting:** AUD $2,000 to $3,000+ in total
- **Extensive or full-mouth grafting:** AUD $4,000 to $5,000+ in total
- **Regenerative periodontal surgery:** AUD $500 to $2,500 per site
- **Flap surgery and advanced periodontal surgery:** AUD $1,000 to $3,000 per quadrant

Remember that these figures describe the general Australian market. A written quote after an examination is the only reliable price for your own mouth, and it is entirely reasonable to ask any clinician for an itemised treatment plan, including item numbers, before you agree to anything. If two quotes differ substantially, ask what accounts for the gap; sometimes it is the clinician's experience, sometimes the technique, and sometimes simply the postcode.

It is easy to look at four-figure numbers and put the whole thing off, but that is usually the most expensive choice of all. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does gum recession surgery cost?

In Australia, gum recession surgery typically costs AUD $600 to $2,000 or more per tooth, while complex or multiple-tooth procedures range from AUD $2,000 to $5,000 or more in total. The final price depends on the severity of the recession, the type of treatment chosen and the specialist's fees.

### Is gum recession surgery covered by insurance?

Private health insurance with extras cover may partially reimburse periodontal surgery, but rebates vary widely by policy and annual limits. Most patients should expect some out-of-pocket expense. Check the relevant item numbers with your insurer before treatment so there are no surprises.

### Is gum recession surgery painful?

The procedure is performed under local anaesthesia, and most patients report only mild discomfort during treatment. Some soreness or sensitivity is common during the recovery period, and your clinician will advise on managing it. Individual experiences vary, so discuss any concerns at your consultation.

### How long does recovery take after gum recession surgery?

Initial healing usually takes one to two weeks, while full stabilisation of the gum tissue can take several weeks to a few months. The exact timeline depends on the type of procedure performed and your individual healing response.

### Can gum recession come back after surgery?

Yes, recurrence is possible if the underlying causes, such as gum disease, aggressive brushing or teeth grinding, are not managed. Good daily oral hygiene and regular dental check-ups are the best protection for your long-term surgical result.
